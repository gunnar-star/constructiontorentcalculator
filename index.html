How the Model Works

This tool answers one specific question: what is the minimum real rent per m² that makes a care home development financially viable in Iceland? It uses an inverted Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) approach — rather than projecting returns from a given rent, it works backwards from a required return to find the rent that exactly satisfies it. All cashflows are modelled in real (inflation-adjusted) terms, which is the natural framework for CPI-indexed leases and verðtryggð lán.

1 · The Core Logic — Inverted DCF

What is an Inverted DCF?

A standard DCF takes a set of inputs including rent and calculates the resulting return. An inverted DCF does the opposite: it sets the required return as the target and asks what rent is needed to achieve it. In this model, the target is Unlevered NPV = 0 at the specified real WACC. The rent that satisfies this is the minimum viable rent in today's money — anything lower and the project does not cover its cost of capital.

Unlevered NPV = −(Construction Cost + Construction Interest) + Σ [ After-Tax CFBDS(t) / (1 + Real WACC)^t ] + Terminal Value (net of CGT) / (1 + Real WACC)^N Solver finds: Rent* such that NPV = 0

The Bisection Solver

The model uses a bisection algorithm starting at ISK 100/m² and ISK 500,000/m², progressively narrowing the range until NPV is within ISK 1 of zero. Converges in under 200 iterations and runs in milliseconds in the browser.

Real WACC must be strictly greater than the Real Exit Cap Rate for the solver to work. If WACC ≤ Exit Cap Rate, the terminal value discounts to more than the initial cost at any positive rent — no minimum can be found. For a development model the exit cap rate is typically below WACC by design: you build at a high yield and sell at a lower yield, with the compression providing the development profit.

2 · Why Real Terms?

Icelandic care home leases are typically CPI-indexed (verðtryggðar leigur). This means rent and operating costs both escalate at the same rate — CPI — so in real terms all cashflows are flat. Modelling in real terms is therefore not a simplification; it is the exact correct framework. The solved rent is the contract rent stated in today's ISK, which is what the lease agreement is signed at. The bank's verðtryggðar lán also behaves cleanly in real terms: the real loan balance is constant and real interest = balance × real rate.

Real WACC ≈ 6.5% for a new development. This is above the 5.5% observed for Reitir and Heimar (Jakobsson Capital, 2026) because their rates reflect seasoned, diversified portfolios. A greenfield single-asset development carries higher construction risk, lease-up risk and illiquidity, justifying a 100–150bp premium.

3 · Construction Financing

During the construction period the bank draws down the construction loan in equal monthly tranches. Interest accrues on the drawn balance throughout the build. Since the average balance is 50% of the total committed loan (drawn evenly from zero to full amount), the total construction interest is:

Construction Interest = CC × LTC × (Rate/12) × Months × 0.5 Example (defaults): 6,000M × 65% × (12%/12) × 20 × 0.5 = 390M ISK

This interest is capitalised — rolled into the permanent loan at handover. The permanent loan opening balance is therefore CC × LTC + Construction Interest, not just CC × LTC. This increases annual debt service throughout the hold period and raises the minimum viable rent. The unlevered NPV also uses the full all-in cost (CC + Construction Interest) as the initial outflow, ensuring both the levered and unlevered analyses are consistent.

The KPI panel shows both the pure construction cost per m² and the all-in cost per m² including capitalised interest, so the development cost basis is always transparent.

4 · Cash Flow Structure

GPI

Gross Potential Income = Annual Rent/m² × NLA. Represents 100% occupancy with no voids. Flat in real terms — CPI escalation cancels out.

EGI

Effective Gross Income = GPI × (1 − Vacancy). Year 1 vacancy reflects lease-up delay (default 25%), Year 2 partial stabilisation (default 5%), fully stabilised from Year 3.

NOI

Net Operating Income = EGI minus all operating expenses: statutory costs, non-recoverable opex (% of EGI), management fee (% of EGI), and Viðhald (maintenance % of CC). Expense recovery is applied to non-statutory costs passed to the tenant.

CFBDS

Cash Flow Before Debt Service = (NOI − Tax) + Depreciation add-back. This is the free cash flow before the lender is paid, used to calculate Unlevered IRR and NPV.

Debt Service

During the interest-only period: interest only on the permanent loan balance. After IO: standard annuity on the real loan balance at the real loan rate (RealBase + Margin). Principal reduces each year.

Terminal Value

At exit: Gross TV = Year N NOI / Real Exit Cap Rate. Net TV = Gross TV × (1 − Exit Costs) − CGT on gain above depreciated book value. The KPI card shows net TV and gross TV for comparison.

5 · Loan Structure — Verðtryggð Lán

The permanent loan is modelled as a real-terms indexed loan (verðtryggð lán), which is standard for large commercial property finance in Iceland. Key mechanics:

ParameterHow it works in the model
Opening balanceCC × LTC + capitalised construction interest
Real loan rateReal Base Rate (RIKS 37 reference) + Lender Margin. Default: 2.54% + 1.10% = 3.64%
Interest-only periodFirst N years: interest only on the full balance. Matches typical bank terms during lease-up.
AmortisationStandard annuity after IO period. Balance declines in real terms each year.
DSCRNOI / Total Debt Service (interest + principal). For IO years: NOI / interest only. Minimum covenant threshold configurable (default 1.10×).

A 1.25× DSCR minimum is a nominal-world convention. For verðtryggð lán, Icelandic banks more commonly assess the Interest Coverage Ratio (ICR = NOI / interest) and watch real LTV against the appraisal. At market defaults the model produces ICR ≈ 1.6–1.8×, which is consistent with bankable sovereign-tenant care home deals.

6 · Iceland Statutory Costs

All statutory costs are computed on the construction cost using standard Icelandic assessment ratios and municipal rates. They are flat in real terms (indexed to property values which track CPI).

Use conservative (high) estimates for bank submissions to stress-test reassessment risk.

Equal construction drawdownLoan drawn in equal monthly tranchesFront-loaded draws (e.g. large land payment) increase interest; adjust CC or construction months accordingly
